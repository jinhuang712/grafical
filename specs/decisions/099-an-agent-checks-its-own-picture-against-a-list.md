# 099. An agent checks its own picture against a list

Decided: 2026-11-08

## The problem

"A picture is judged by how it reads" says the owner's eye reads a picture, and no owner sits beside an agent in real use. The guideline on answers said the agent judges from the counts. On 2026-11-07 a `pyramid` render passed on crossings alone, while its bends, its width and its mixed sides were all in the records: the fault was that only one number was checked. The list of what makes a good picture sat in the goals, where no agent reads it.

## The choice

- The owner's eye judges grafical, on the routing cases and the drafts of a round.
- An agent judges its own picture, by looking at it and reading the records.
- The points of a good picture go into `grafical guide composition`, written for an agent.
- The steps of `grafical --help` end by telling the agent to look at the picture and check it against that guide before it stops.
- grafical still judges nothing: it gives the list, and the agent decides.

## Not chosen

- The list in the guide with nothing pointing to it.
  - An agent that never opens the guide checks one number, as happened on 2026-11-07.
- The agent told only to look at the picture.
  - A look with no list can stop at one fault.
- The counts and warnings alone.
  - Some points need the picture, such as whether the structure shows at once.
- The list printed in the help.
  - A rule in words before any mistake is what "An agent learns a tool by using it" holds against, and it would reach every agent at length.

## Consequences

This reverses in part "Help for the tool, guides for best practice", where nothing told an agent to read a guide, and its test changes. One line of the help is close to a rule in words: it says when to check, and the rules stay in the guide. Whether a small model can see these faults is not measured. An agent looks at least once in a draft and in a change, with no upper limit, so the cost of looking shows in time and tokens and not in a limit on looks.
